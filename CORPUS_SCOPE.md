# Corpus Scope and Boundary Statement

## Project

**British Ghost Corpus: Deduplicated Translations**  
Version 0.3 · 318 active texts · 1764–1913

This corpus was constructed for Jong-Keyong Kim’s dissertation, *Gothic Ontography: Changing Materialism in British Ghost Story, 1764–1913*. It is a researcher-built collection rather than a comprehensive or neutral representation of all ghost writing circulating in Britain during the period.

## Historical frame

The corpus uses 1764, the publication year of Horace Walpole’s *The Castle of Otranto*, as its opening boundary and 1913 as its closing boundary. These dates support the dissertation’s account of the long development of British Gothic ghost writing across changing print, material, economic, and technological conditions.

## Operational meaning of “British”

“British” describes participation in British literary and print circulation rather than author nationality alone. A text may enter the corpus through one or more of the following relations:

- authorship by a British or British-associated writer;
- first or significant publication in Britain;
- English-language circulation within British periodicals, books, anthologies, or publishing networks;
- translation incorporated into British literary circulation;
- colonial or transnational material mediated through British print culture;
- later inclusion in British ghost-story collections relevant to the corpus genealogy.

These categories are recorded as provenance relations where evidence is available. Inclusion does not erase a text’s original national, linguistic, colonial, or cultural location.

## Eligible textual units

Eligible units may include complete novels and novellas, short fiction, separately titled contributions in collections, poetry, drama, essays, testimony-like anecdotes, folklore or archive records, English translations, hoaxes, ambiguous apparitions, animal ghosts, and other substantial postmortem presences.

A chapter mechanically extracted from a unitary novel is not treated as an independent work. Separately titled and independently circulating tales may be treated as distinct units when the source structure supports that decision.

## Supernatural boundary

A text is not included merely because it uses the words *ghost*, *spectre*, or *apparition*. The corpus ordinarily excludes works whose sole supernatural agent is a vampire, demon, double, fairy or Sidhe, werewolf, witchcraft mechanism, metaphorical specter, or other non-postmortem entity. Borderline cases are retained or excluded through documented researcher adjudication rather than keyword matching.

## Corpus development

The auditable corpus developed through successive stages of discovery, comparison screening, gender recovery, diversity recovery, canonical and boundary review, duplicate removal, and translation deduplication. The active count changed from 175 to 237, 285, 317, 320, 319, and finally 318 texts.

The recovery passes were corrective interventions, not claims of demographic completeness. They made underrepresentation more visible and changed the corpus’s analytical possibilities, but they did not remove the biases of digitization, survival, access, attribution, or researcher selection.

## Current limitations

Metadata completeness varies. Some files are clean public-domain transcriptions; others derive from OCR and require verification against page images before quotation. Some author, date, venue, or source fields remain unresolved. The corpus should therefore be used with the manifest, provenance notes, and quality flags rather than as an unqualified body of equivalent texts.
