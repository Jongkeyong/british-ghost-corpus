# Data, Rights, and Responsible-Use Statement

## Status of this repository

This repository documents the construction and use of the **British Ghost Corpus: Deduplicated Translations, Version 0.3 (318 active texts)**. The repository is currently private and intended for dissertation review and controlled scholarly collaboration.

## Rights and redistribution

No single license can be applied automatically to all primary texts represented by the corpus. Individual works, editions, transcriptions, scans, translations, OCR outputs, and repository exports may carry different legal and contractual conditions.

Accordingly:

- copyright remains with the relevant authors, translators, editors, publishers, repositories, or other rights holders;
- the presence of metadata in this repository does not imply permission to redistribute the corresponding full text;
- public-domain status of an original work does not necessarily establish the status of a modern edition, translation, transcription, or repository-generated file;
- full texts should be uploaded only after source-specific rights and terms have been reviewed;
- where redistribution is uncertain, the manifest should provide a stable source link and provenance note instead of reproducing the text.

The documentation, metadata authored for this project, and original code may later receive explicit licenses after review. Until then, no permission beyond ordinary private committee access should be inferred.

## Data layers

The project distinguishes among:

1. **Bibliographic and descriptive metadata** created or normalized for the corpus;
2. **Provenance records** documenting source, retrieval, edition, translation, and transformation history;
3. **Primary-text files** whose rights and quality vary;
4. **Derived data** such as counts, feature tables, classifications, network relations, and visualizations;
5. **Research documentation** including screening rules, exclusions, uncertainties, prompts, model information, and revision history.

Each release should state which layers are present and which remain withheld.

## Bias and representativeness

The corpus is not a census of British ghost writing. It reflects survival, digitization, repository access, searchability, anthologization, bibliographic knowledge, language, attribution, and researcher judgment. Corrective recovery passes increased the representation of women writers and other underrepresented transmission routes, but they do not establish demographic completeness.

Users should not treat corpus frequencies as direct measurements of the historical literary field without accounting for the selection process.

## OCR and textual quality

Some records derive from OCR and may contain damaged words, lost punctuation, incorrect paragraphing, or source-boundary errors. Quality flags must accompany computational use. Quotations, names, dates, and interpretively significant passages should be verified against scans or authoritative editions.

## AI-assisted research

AI systems assisted portions of retrieval, comparison, documentation, and methodological formalization. Their outputs were subject to researcher review and revision. AI assistance does not transfer authorship of the project’s scholarly argument or final corpus decisions away from the researcher.

Any released computational results should preserve, where applicable:

- model and provider name;
- model version or access date;
- prompt or structured instruction;
- deterministic post-processing rules;
- corpus and rubric version;
- known failures and human corrections.

## Privacy and sensitive information

The corpus concerns historical published materials and ordinarily contains no human-subject data. Working notes may nevertheless include private correspondence, credentials, committee-only drafts, or repository access information. Such material must not be committed.

## Recommended use

This repository is appropriate for methodological review, corpus genealogy, metadata inspection, reproducibility assessment, and the development of historically responsible computational literary analysis. It should not be used to claim exhaustive coverage, uniform textual reliability, or legally unrestricted access to every represented work.
