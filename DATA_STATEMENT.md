# Data, Rights, and Responsible-Use Statement

## 1. Repository status

This repository documents the construction and use of the **British Ghost Corpus: Deduplicated Translations, Version 0.3 (318 active texts)**. It is presently a private, committee-facing research release. Public release, licensing, and DOI registration remain separate future decisions.

## 2. What the corpus represents

The corpus is a researcher-constructed sample, not a census of British ghost writing between 1764 and 1913. Its composition reflects textual survival, digitization, repository access, searchability, anthology history, bibliographic knowledge, language, attribution, technical extractability, and researcher judgment.

“British” is used primarily as a circulation category rather than as a claim that every included author was British. Relevant routes may include British or Irish authorship, first publication in Britain, English-language publication, circulation within British print culture, colonial or translated material incorporated into that circulation, and later British anthology transmission. Individual records should identify the applicable route when verified.

## 3. Construction and recovery

The corpus developed through successive stages of discovery, screening, repair, recovery, canonical expansion, and deduplication. Researcher-directed recovery passes increased the representation of women writers, Charlotte Riddell, collaborative authorship, colonial circulation, and translated or retold materials. These interventions corrected visible imbalances in the working collection; they did not establish demographic completeness.

Users should therefore avoid treating corpus frequencies as direct measurements of the historical literary field. Apparent absence may reflect archival and digitization conditions rather than historical nonexistence.

## 4. Inclusion and exclusion

Eligible textual units may include complete novels and novellas, separately titled contributions in collections, short fiction, poetry, drama, essays, testimony-like anecdotes, folklore records, English translations, hoaxes, ambiguous apparitions, animal ghosts, and other substantial postmortem presences.

Chapters mechanically extracted from a unitary novel are not independent corpus items. Vampires, demons, doubles, fairies or Sidhe, werewolves, and metaphor-only specters are ordinarily excluded when they constitute the sole supernatural agent. Borderline decisions remain subject to documented literary adjudication rather than keyword presence alone.

## 5. Data layers

The project distinguishes among:

1. **Bibliographic and descriptive metadata** created or normalized for the corpus;
2. **Provenance records** documenting source, retrieval, edition, translation, and transformation history;
3. **Primary-text witnesses** whose rights and textual quality vary;
4. **Derived data** such as word counts, feature tables, classifications, network relations, rankings, and visualizations; and
5. **Research documentation** including criteria, exclusions, uncertainties, prompts, model information, and revision history.

Each release should state which layers are included and which remain withheld.

## 6. Manifest verification

The Version 0.3 text package contains 318 active UTF-8 plain-text witnesses numbered BGH001–BGH320, with BGH018 and BGH213 absent by design. The manifest records file-level identifiers, filenames, word counts, byte counts, and SHA-256 checksums. A checksum verifies file identity only; it does not establish bibliographic accuracy, textual authority, or interpretive validity.

Unverified metadata are left blank or marked for review. Missing dates, source URLs, translator identities, and edition details must not be silently inferred.

## 7. OCR and textual quality

Some witnesses derive from OCR or from mechanically extracted source containers. They may contain damaged words, altered punctuation, incorrect paragraphing, duplicate paratext, or boundary errors. Computational analysis should retain quality flags and distinguish source text from cleaned derivatives.

Quotations, names, publication dates, and interpretively consequential passages should be checked against scans or authoritative editions before publication.

## 8. Rights and redistribution

No single license can automatically govern every primary text represented by the corpus. Individual works, editions, transcriptions, scans, translations, OCR outputs, and repository exports may carry different legal or contractual conditions.

Accordingly:

- copyright remains with the relevant authors, translators, editors, publishers, repositories, or other rights holders;
- metadata presence does not imply permission to redistribute the corresponding full text;
- public-domain status of an original work does not necessarily establish the status of a modern edition, translation, transcription, or repository-generated file;
- primary texts should be deposited only after source-specific rights and terms have been reviewed; and
- when redistribution is uncertain, a record should provide provenance and a stable source link rather than reproduce the text.

Unless a file states otherwise, no open license should be inferred for the primary-text corpus.

## 9. AI-assisted research

AI systems assisted portions of retrieval, comparison, documentation, genre formalization, and application design. Their outputs were reviewed and revised by the researcher. AI assistance does not transfer responsibility for the project’s scholarly arguments, corpus boundaries, or final adjudications away from the researcher.

Released AI-assisted results should preserve, where applicable:

- provider and model name;
- model version or access date;
- prompt, rubric, or structured instruction;
- deterministic post-processing rules;
- corpus, feature, and rubric versions;
- known failures; and
- human corrections or disagreements.

## 10. Computational interpretation

Statistical deviation is not treated as literary anomaly by itself. Rankings and visualizations are instruments for directing attention. Their interpretation requires comparison with historical context, textual form, corpus construction, feature design, model limitations, and close reading.

Visualizations should display uncertainty and constructedness rather than present modeled relations as observer-independent facts.

## 11. Privacy and security

The corpus concerns historical published materials and ordinarily contains no human-subject data. Working directories may nevertheless contain private correspondence, credentials, committee-only drafts, or repository access information. Such material must not be committed.

## 12. Recommended use

This repository is appropriate for methodological review, corpus genealogy, metadata inspection, reproducibility assessment, computational experimentation, and historically responsible literary analysis. It should not be used to claim exhaustive coverage, uniform textual reliability, culturally neutral genre criteria, or legally unrestricted access to every represented work.
