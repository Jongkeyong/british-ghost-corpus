# Ghost Story Lens Documentation

This directory contains the principal documentation for *Ghost Story Lens*, a research prototype developed alongside the *British Ghost Corpus*. The application emerged from a recurring interpretive problem encountered during corpus construction: how to determine, consistently and transparently, whether a literary text should count as a ghost story.

The documents collected here record different stages of that development, from the formulation and testing of the classification criteria to the design and technical specification of the application. Together, they provide an auditable record of how literary-critical judgments were translated into explicit criteria, computational procedures, and interface features.

## Documents

### `Criteria development report.pdf`

Documents the development, testing, and revision of the criteria used to determine whether a text qualifies as a ghost story. It records the interpretive problems and boundary cases that motivated revisions to the rubric and provides methodological context for the criteria implemented in *Ghost Story Lens*.

### `Ghost_Story_Lens_Project_Development_Report.md`

Provides a genealogy of the project from its origins in the construction of the *British Ghost Corpus* through the development of *Ghost Story Lens*. It explains how problems of corpus inclusion and exclusion led to the formulation of an explicit ghost-story rubric and subsequently to the conception of an application that could make those interpretive decisions inspectable and revisable.

### `Ghost Story Lens_Design Rationale and Build Intent.pdf`

Explains the scholarly and design rationale behind the application, including its relationship to literary interpretation, critical making, human review, evidentiary transparency, and the visualization of relations among ghosts, mediators, and ghost-seers.

### `Ghost_Story_Lens_PRD_v1_4.pdf`

The Product Requirements Document (PRD), version 1.4. It specifies the application's intended functions, analytical workflow, interface requirements, data structures, reporting features, and implementation requirements.

## Documentation Sequence

These documents can be read as a record of the project's development:

**corpus classification problem → criteria development and testing → project conception → design rationale → application specification**

The sequence is not intended to imply that development was strictly linear. Criteria, design decisions, and implementation requirements were revised iteratively as boundary cases and application tests exposed limitations in earlier formulations.

## Research Transparency

These materials are preserved as part of the project's methodological record. *Ghost Story Lens* does not treat computational classification as self-validating evidence. Its criteria, scores, extracted evidence, relational maps, and reports remain objects of human interpretation and revision. Making the development documents available alongside the corpus documentation allows readers to examine not only the application's outputs but also the decisions through which its analytical categories and procedures were constructed.
