# British Ghost Corpus v0.4: canonical and boundary expansion report

## Release outcome

- 320 unique corpus texts: 317 inherited from v0.3 plus three newly ingested works.
- Oscar Wilde's *The Canterville Ghost* was already present as BGH187. Its inclusion was reconfirmed and its publication metadata strengthened; it was not duplicated.
- New full texts: Horace Walpole's *The Castle of Otranto* (BGH318), Walter Scott's “My Aunt Margaret's Mirror” (BGH319), and Matthew Gregory Lewis's *The Monk: A Romance* (BGH320).
- *The Monk* is explicitly marked as a user-directed boundary inclusion because its Bleeding Nun revenant is embedded in a novel whose larger supernatural machinery is substantially demonic.

## Authorship coding

## Methodological consequence

This release deliberately distinguishes corpus inclusion from taxonomic purity. Walpole and Scott strengthen the corpus's account of materially mediated apparition: armour, portrait, architecture, mirror, ritual book, and reflected scene act as ontographic relays. Lewis is retained as a named boundary work, allowing the chapter to compare a discrete revenant episode with a novel dominated by diabolical agency without silently weakening the corpus's ordinary demon exclusion.

The 317-text v0.3 diversity-recovery layer remains intact. Because the three new authors are men and canonical Gothic writers, v0.4 slightly reduces the proportional representation of women while improving chronological and genealogical coverage. Author-capped and layer-sensitive analyses remain necessary.

## Text provenance and fitness

The three new texts use public-domain Project Gutenberg transcriptions. Repository license boilerplate was removed. Henry Morley's later editorial introduction was also removed from *The Castle of Otranto*, while Walpole's own prefaces were retained. Scott's story frame and Lewis's authorial preface remain part of their autonomous works. Quotations should still be checked against the historical edition cited in the dissertation.
