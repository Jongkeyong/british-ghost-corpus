# Methodology

## Research purpose

The British Ghost Corpus supports a dissertation argument about haunting as distributed material and relational agency. Corpus construction was therefore guided not only by lexical presence but by whether a text offered a narratively substantial relation among a postmortem presence, forms of mediation, perceivers, objects, spaces, documents, bodies, atmospheres, economic instruments, or technologies.

## Construction workflow

The corpus was built iteratively rather than collected in a single pass.

1. **Discovery collection.** Candidate texts were gathered from digital repositories, anthologies, bibliographies, and source containers. The earliest fully auditable package contains 175 items.
2. **Comparison screening.** A second collection was compared against the discovery set. Sixty-two usable, nonduplicative works were added, producing 237 items.
3. **Gender-recovery pass.** The researcher identified an implausibly low representation of nineteenth-century women writers and directed a corrective search and screening process. Forty-eight works were added, producing 285 items.
4. **Diversity-recovery pass.** Additional attention to underrepresented authors and transmission routes added thirty-two works, producing 317 items. This stage included further Charlotte Riddell texts, E. and H. Heron collaborations, and tales mediated by Lal Behari Day.
5. **Canonical and boundary review.** The researcher tested conspicuous absences and difficult Gothic cases. Three new works were added; a requested Wilde text was found already present. The corpus reached 320 records.
6. **Integrity audit.** One near-exact duplicate and one alternate English translation of an already represented Maupassant story were removed from the active set. The current active corpus contains 318 texts. Excluded witnesses are retained in provenance records rather than silently deleted.

## Researcher adjudication

Automated and AI-assisted processes supported retrieval, comparison, metadata inspection, and provisional screening. They did not independently determine the corpus boundary. Final decisions were controlled by the researcher’s historical, generic, and interpretive judgments.

The researcher’s interventions included:

- establishing the chronological frame;
- defining the difference between ghost stories and broader supernatural fiction;
- rejecting keyword presence as a sufficient criterion;
- deciding how to treat ambiguous apparitions, hoaxes, crisis doubles, animal ghosts, translations, folklore records, and independently titled units;
- directing corrective recovery for women writers and other underrepresented routes of circulation;
- testing canonical absences;
- retaining uncertainty and failed retrievals in the project record;
- distinguishing duplicate files from distinct translations, editions, or witnesses.

## Screening principles

A candidate was evaluated through a combination of bibliographic evidence, source structure, textual inspection, and narrative function. Relevant questions included:

- Is there a once-living or plausibly postmortem presence?
- Does that presence materially affect plot, knowledge, bodies, property, space, or interpretation?
- Is the apparition central, sustained, or structurally meaningful rather than metaphorical or incidental?
- Does the source preserve a complete and independently meaningful textual unit?
- Is the apparent entity better described as a vampire, demon, fairy, double, hallucination, fraud, or another adjacent form?
- Does the item duplicate another file, represent a distinct edition, or preserve a separate translation?

Borderline decisions should be read with the screening and exclusion logs when those files are released.

## Textual integrity and OCR

Text quality is heterogeneous. Files may originate from manually prepared transcriptions, repository text exports, or OCR. OCR-derived texts are suitable for exploratory computational analysis only when their quality flags are considered. Quotations and fine-grained close readings must be checked against authoritative scans or editions.

Cleaning should preserve meaningful punctuation, paragraph boundaries, headings, and textual anomalies where possible. It should not silently modernize spelling or rewrite damaged passages. Every substantive repair should be documented.

## Translation deduplication

The current release name, **Deduplicated Translations**, indicates that alternate English translations of the same source work are not automatically treated as separate literary works in the active analytic set. Distinct translations remain important textual witnesses and should be retained in provenance storage. The active set selects one representative translation when analysis at the level of separate translation histories is not the research question.

## Reproducibility standard

A reproducible release should preserve:

- a stable corpus identifier for each active record;
- author and title strings as used in the project;
- normalized and original metadata where available;
- source and retrieval information;
- inclusion, exclusion, and uncertainty notes;
- duplicate and translation relations;
- text-quality and OCR flags;
- versioned manifests and changelogs;
- scripts, prompts, parameters, and model versions used in later computational analysis.

Reproducibility does not mean that every literary judgment becomes objective. It means that the evidence, categories, interventions, uncertainties, and revisions through which the corpus was made remain inspectable.
